# What happened next

The brief stops at what was public by March 31, 2023. This page uses later filings, through August 2026, to check it. Script: `src/epilogue.py` → `outputs/epilogue.csv`, `outputs/epilogue_gaps.csv`.

Latest filed values. Target restated some FY2022 and FY2023 costs in 2025, moving $77M and $92M from SG&A into cost of goods. FY2022's operating income didn't change.

## How the brief held up

- **Cost of goods was the right place to look for FY2022.** The rate came back 3.4 of the 4.7 points it had risen by FY2025, though not to 70.7%.
- **Inventory eased as costs did.** Target's inventory grew more slowly than sales in all four quarters of FY2023, and the cost rate fell 3.0 points that year. That fits the overstock explanation but doesn't prove it: freight and other costs may have eased too.
- **Where the brief was too comfortable: SG&A.** It treated running costs as a small part of the drop. The SG&A rate then rose 1.7 points from FY2022 to FY2025. Against FY2021, Target's FY2025 margin is 3.6 points lower: SG&A explains 1.9 of it and cost of goods 1.4.
- **Revenue didn't keep growing.** It fell 4.0% from FY2022 to FY2025.

## How the trigger held up

Over the 14 quarters from Q1 FY2023 to Q2 FY2026, Target's gap passed 10 twice (10.3 in Q4 FY2024 and 14.0 in Q1 FY2025) and never passed 15. The "look" level fired and the "review" level didn't. Over FY2025 the cost rate rose only 0.3 points and operating margin fell 0.3, so those flags look like mild signals at most. Walmart's gap never passed 10, and Kohl's did once.
